Hill Workouts & Route Strategy

Build the climb first, then the descent. Downhill running causes most of the muscle damage in hilly marathons, so practice it on purpose.

Base · weeks 5–16

Short hill repeats (45–60 s)

4–5% grade, 6–8 reps at 5K–10K effort, jog down fully recovered. Focus on tall posture and quick cadence.

Block · weeks 1–7

800 m hill repeats

5–6 reps at 10K–half marathon effort on a 3–5% grade. Jog back down. Last rep should match the first.

Block · weeks 5–11

Sustained 1-mile climbs

2–3 climbs of one mile at steady-hard effort (about marathon effort plus 20–30 s/mi), then cruise the descent relaxed. This is the Golden Gate Bridge approach workout.

Block · weeks 7–13

Down-and-control repeats

3–4 x 0.3 mi downhill at marathon effort with short steps and a slight forward lean. Builds quad durability for SF’s long descents.

Block · weeks 10–13

Goal-pace rolling run

6 miles at 10:40–10:55/mi on rolling roads. Hold effort on the climbs, let pace recover on the descents.

Route type

The “Bridge Approach” climb

A long, steady 0.7–1.2 mi road or greenway climb at 3–5%. Use it for sustained 1-mile climbs. Overpass or levee-top approaches work if no continuous hill is nearby.

Route type

Golden Gate Park rollers

A looping park route (like a 5-mile scenic drive loop) with constant 20–60 ft rises and drops. Ideal for long runs, easy hilly days and goal-pace rolling work.

Route type

Neighborhood punch hills

Short steep streets (8–12%) for strides and power reps. Walk the steepest bit if needed; as you noted on your Aug 2 run, walking up hills isn’t a failure.

Winter / bad weather

Treadmill hill session

Use 4–6% incline for 3–5 min climbs, then 0–1% recovery. Do the downhill practice outdoors, since treadmills barely train eccentric load.

Elevation targets for long runs

Match the course profile

The race is roughly 65 ft per mile. Peak long runs target 65–85 ft/mi, for example about 1,600–1,700 ft in a 20-miler. Your best so far is 669 ft in 5.8 mi, so the capacity is already there.

Check the official course map before race week; routes and aid stations change year to year.

Recovery & Race Prep

Most of your fitness gets built in the recovery between runs. With only 3 run days a week, the off days matter as much as the workouts.

Every week

Recovery basics

  • Sleep: aim for 7–9 hours, and protect the night before Sunday long runs.
  • Post-run fueling: eat carbs plus protein within about an hour of hard sessions and long runs.
  • Easy means easy: Thursday runs and long runs stay conversational so Tuesday and Sunday can be your hard days.
  • Cutback weeks: every 4th week drops volume on purpose. Don’t skip them.
  • Day after long run: walk or easy spin for 20–30 minutes instead of resting completely.
Strength · 2×/week, 15–25 min

Leg strength for hills and descents

  • Split squats or reverse lunges: 3×8 per leg
  • Step-ups onto a bench: 3×10 per leg (your hill climbing muscles)
  • Single-leg calf raises: 3×12, with slow lowering
  • Glute bridges or hip thrusts: 3×12
  • Side planks and dead bugs: 2×30 s per side
  • Descent prep (block phase): slow eccentric step-downs, 3×8 per leg.

Do strength on Mon, Wed or Fri, not before Tue hills or Sun long runs. Keep it light the week before a 20-miler.

Daily, 5–10 min

Mobility

Ankle and calf mobility, hip flexor and hamstring stretches, and foam rolling for quads and calves. Hill running loads the calves and Achilles hard, so give them extra attention.

Red flags

When to back off

  • Pain that changes how you run, or gets worse during a run
  • Sharp pain in the shin, foot, hip or heel (possible stress injury)
  • Achilles or knee pain after hills or descents that lasts more than 2 days
  • Resting heart rate up and mood or sleep off for several days

Swap that run for a bike ride, or take a rest day. If pain persists, see a sports medicine clinician before the next hard week.

Gear

Shoes and kit

  • Rotate two pairs of shoes and replace them around 300–500 miles.
  • Buy your race shoes by mid-May so you have 100+ miles in them before race day.
  • Wear your exact race kit on at least two long runs. Test shorts, socks, a hat or visor and anti-chafe balm.
  • SF mornings are cool, foggy and windy near the bridge. Plan removable layers such as arm sleeves and a throwaway top for the start.
Fueling

Practice race-day nutrition

  • On runs over 75 minutes, practice taking about 30–60 g of carbs per hour. Build up gradually toward what your stomach handles.
  • Use the same gels or chews the race provides, if possible, and test them in training.
  • Drink to thirst, add electrolytes on long or warm runs, and don’t try anything new on race morning.
